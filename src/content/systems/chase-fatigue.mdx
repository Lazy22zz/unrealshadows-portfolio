## The problem

Players are caught on contact and cannot kill anything. If a chase never ends, the only exit is to break line of sight — and a room with six to eight guards has no blind spots. A per-guard meter does not work either: when one guard tires, a fresh one takes over immediately ("infinite relay"). The first fix attached fatigue to the *pursuit* of each player instead — but when that meter filled, every chaser gave up on the same frame, which read as a switch flipping. The current version keeps the per-player heat and adds per-chaser stamina so they drop out one at a time.

## What the player sees

- Icons above guards: `?` suspicious, `!` spotted, chasing, searching, sweating, panting — never a stamina bar. (A 3+1 tier redesign of these icons is specified but not implemented yet.)
- Chasers tire one by one; the last one stops, then the followers drop off 0–1.5 s apart.
- A red ink ring under your feet points at whoever is chasing you (a separate, local-only component).

## Why this design

- **Memory is not sight.** Only a real perception lock may *continue* a pursuit. A remembered last-seen point does not, or fatigue would never resolve.
- **Ask per controller.** Sweating and speed come from the chaser's own stamina, not the player's meter — otherwise every guard would start sweating on the same frame.
- **Screen-space widgets see through walls by default,** so the head icon hides beyond 25 m or behind geometry. That gate exists to prevent wall-hacking, not for looks.
- **Rollback is one flag**: `ChaseFatigue.bStaggeredStamina = false` restores the earlier all-at-once behaviour.

## Limits and next steps

- The old team "pressure thermometer" and its three-stage music were retired in this redesign; music now follows the pursuit state only.
- Crowded-room tuning still needs human playtests.
