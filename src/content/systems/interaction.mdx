## The problem

The game has dozens of things you press **E** on: switches, sealed doors, task NPCs, a coin lock, relics, rescue consoles, warded chests. They need the same feel (short press or hold, a prompt that tells you what will happen) but very different rules about *when* they work. Two classes of bug kept recurring:

1. **"I press E and nothing happens."** The target could not be used, but the input still started a 1.5 s hold bar with no label.
2. **Exploits through geometry.** The sweep starts 1 m in front of the capsule. Standing against bars, the start point was already on the other side, and the server only checked distance — so a sword could be pulled through a grille.

## What the player sees

<Figure id="ui-denied" caption="Glyph prompt: conditions that are not met shake red and the hold never starts. Conditions that are met are drawn in gold." />

- A glyph prompt (action icon, one or two words, lock and condition icons).
- If something is locked, pressing E either shakes the missing conditions or, for a few targets, makes the player say one short line (e.g. which coin is still missing).
- Holds are reserved for actions that really happen.

The decision of what a key press does is a pure function, so the "zero regression for the other implementers" promise can be asserted in a test:

<Snippet id="interact-resolve" strip caption="Only 'cannot interact AND has something to say' changes behaviour. Without a refusal line the old path is kept, because e.g. an NPC cooldown can legitimately expire during the 1.5 s hold." />

Refusal text is an optional `BlueprintNativeEvent` on the interface with an empty default, so 7 of the 9 implementers did not change at all. Refusal is **local only**: the facts it reads (`bLocked`, coin progress) are already replicated, and a server round trip would only delay feedback that should be instant.

On the server, `Interact_Server` re-checks distance **and** line of sight from the capsule centre to the target; hitting the target itself or anything attached to it (a sword's pedestal) is accepted.

| Piece | Responsibility |

| `LineOfSightAccepts` | Pure rule for the server LOS check (target or its attachment chain) |
| `AUS_TrialSwitch` | Reusable one-shot lever; `LinkedTargets` opens doors, activates portals, unlocks sealed doors |

## Why this design

- **One judgement, two callers.** `CanInteract` and the commit path must use the same predicate. This was learned twice: once with a sealed door, once with artifacts whose hold bar ran a full second and then did nothing. Artifacts now share `ResolveRealRefusal()` between both.
- **Refusal is cosmetic, authority is not.** The client can only make things *quieter*; it can never make a locked thing open.
- **Watch for replication races.** The coin altar's lock lives on the actor; coin progress lives on the GameState. A client can see "both coins collected" before "altar unlocked". In that window the altar stays silent instead of telling the player a lie (`USCoinLock::ResolvePrompt(true, true) == None`, covered by a test).

<Callout kind="warn" title="A sweep blind spot worth knowing">
The sweep starts 100 cm in front of the body and covers roughly ±50 cm vertically. Interactables on tall pedestals or in wall niches get no prompt at all — once, four artifacts were unclickable for exactly this reason. Placement now accounts for it.
</Callout>

## Limits and next steps

- The sweep follows the body's facing, not the camera; scripted clients must turn the pawn before pressing.
- Gamepad-only interaction flows were not part of the verification matrix.
