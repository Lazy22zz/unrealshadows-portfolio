## The brief

The player's brief was short: the face should be **two layers**. Going for the artifact you walk the floor, where a guard patrols. Going for the sword you must walk **along the shelf tops**, and the tops need detours, dead ends, and reasons to climb down and work a mechanism that moves the shelves.

## Design rules

- **The whole face is bookcases.** The first version kept a straight diagonal wall and put a small puzzle in one corner; it was rejected as "blunt". In the current version the two halves are separated only by how the cases join up: a dividing line of shelves (`PW`, `E1`, `PE`, `PEE`) runs from the west wall to the east wall, and its top is the upper layer's main road.
- **A rolling case is a bridge on top and a wall on the floor.** Every configuration keeps the floor split into a sword side and an artifact side (switch the demo to *Floor* and try all eight).
- **Both stops are real paths; some turns open nothing.** That is what makes the order matter: the fewest turns from the landing is **{lm.minTurns}**, with exactly {lm.orders.length} valid orders.
- **Measured gaps, not guessed ones.** A sprinting body clears a 2 m gap about every other try (there is no jump; the capsule's rounded bottom hooks the far edge), never 4 m, and never one guarded by an 80 cm parapet. So two open edges never face each other within 3 m.
- **Classes may not skip it.** Shelf tops sit inside a blink ward, so the thief's knife-teleport is refused there, and the thief's step height was normalised to everyone else's.

<Callout kind="warn" title="A pocket the solver found">
Extending one row of cases on the artifact side turned the aisle at the foot of the stairs into a dead pocket: once down, you could no longer reach winch W3. The solver flood-fills the floor for all eight configurations, so this showed up as a failed check before anything was built.
</Callout>

## From solver to shipped map

<Gallery ids={["library-pad", "library-top", "library-lane", "library-winch"]} cols={2} />

1. `LibraryMazeLayout.py` holds every piece, stop and winch station, and checks **97 rules** in a few seconds.
2. `ApplyLibraryMaze.py` builds a candidate map from the formal map; a walking bot follows the solver's own winch order in `-game`.
3. After playtests (thief fairness, guard patrols), the patch was written to the formal `Level_02` behind a file-hash guard.
4. Art and sound (wordless markers — quill, ink bottle, candle, ladder — iron rails, winch sounds) were added afterwards as no-collision dressing, and a geometry comparison confirmed that no colliding piece moved.

Results recorded on the shipped map: walking bot **79/79** (warrior), **82/82** (thief), **79/79** (duo roster); five gravity lanes with zero blockers.
