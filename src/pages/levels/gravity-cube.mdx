## Reading the cube

- **Six rooms, six gravities.** The arrows show "down" in each room. Walking up to an edge carries you over a seam onto the next face.
- **Lanes connect far faces.** A drain on the west wall flings you along +X into an iron trough on the east wall; a grate outside the spawn door launches you up onto the ceiling's shelves; the zenith shaft drops you through the whole cube.
- **The core.** Four swords are spread over the cube. Drawing enough of them (three solo, four in a duo) slides the heavenly core under the zenith shaft; step into the shaft and you fall onto its deck, where the time gate to the temple waits inside a shield.

## Building it

The cube is generated by `BuildStage2GravityCube.py` (full editor Python), then refined by narrow, hash-guarded patch scripts. For every colliding piece on a wall or the ceiling the generator also creates a **nav-twin** copy laid flat 600 m below the level, where guards path-find; the AI maps the result back onto the face.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Edges are where geometry fights">
Two faces' 9 m bands overlap at every edge, panels must extend past the edge to close the corner pillar, and anything near a seam arc must keep out of it. <code>AnalyseStage2Clipping.py</code> runs oriented-box overlap tests on an exported dump (43 → 0 conflicts in the clean-up pass) and sweeps the five gravity lanes for blockers.
</Callout>

## What the 3-D view shows

Everything is drawn from a geometry dump of the shipped map: collision boxes are instanced from their real centres and half-axes, rooms and lanes from their `AUS_GravityZone` frames, landmarks from actor positions. It does not show meshes, lighting or the art pass — see the screenshots for that. More on the code side: <a href={u("/systems/gravity-rooms/")}>gravity rooms and seams</a>.
