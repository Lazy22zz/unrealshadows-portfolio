## How it plays

Energy runs **source → disc I → junction → disc II → gate seal**. Each disc carries one groove from a U-shaped inlet to a spout on the opposite side; the target position is visible on purpose — the difficulty is mechanical, not hidden information:

- The two discs normally turn independently.
- Lowering the **clutch** (two idler gears) links them: turning one turns the other **the opposite way** (two idlers in series reverse the direction).
- **Disc II is seized.** It can only move when disc I drags it through the clutch.

So the intended solution is: engage → use disc I to drag disc II into place → disengage → fix disc I on its own. Forget to disengage and fixing disc I drags disc II right back out.

## The iteration that created it

The first version had the gears permanently meshed. Players reported **"when I turn the left disc, the right one turns too"** as a bug. Investigation found two causes stacked: the rule itself, and a real presentation bug — one global animation timer made *both* discs wobble, and disc II's teeth and groove were drawn with different angle sources, so meshed discs appeared to turn the *same* way. The redesign kept the linkage but made it a choice (the clutch) and gave it a purpose (the seized disc).

- **The clutch RPC sends a target value, not "toggle".** Two players pressing Space at the same moment would cancel each other's toggles; target values are idempotent.
- **Seized rejection is predicted locally.** The widget and the server call the same `CanDrive`, which only reads replicated state, so the local shake and the server's refusal always agree and no RPC is wasted.
- **`LastDriver` is replicated only for the animation layer**, so the dragged disc can start a beat later than the driving one.
- **Puzzles are generated, not authored.** `BuildPuzzle` keeps both discs off target, the clutch free, and requires at least five optimal moves in the shipped table.

<Callout kind="info" title="Checked against the engine test">
The automation test <code>UnrealShadows.Stage1.CoinLock.Rules</code> compares a production breadth-first search against an independent reference over all 36 × 36 × clutch × seized states and pins the worst cases at 4, 5 and 7 moves. The site's own Vitest suite asserts the same three numbers on the TypeScript port.
